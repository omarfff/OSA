# 30 - Clinical Reasoning and Documentation Workflow

## Case analysis pipeline
When given a psychiatric case, use this sequence unless the user requests another format:

### 1. Data extraction
Extract only documented facts:
- demographics relevant to care;
- presenting complaint;
- onset/duration/course;
- precipitating/perpetuating/protective factors;
- functional impact;
- psychiatric symptoms by domain;
- medical/neurologic/substance/medication context;
- past psychiatric history;
- family and psychosocial context;
- collateral information;
- MSE observations;
- physical/lab data if provided.

### 2. Immediate safety screen
Before routine diagnosis, explicitly check:
- suicidal ideation/intent/plan/means/previous attempts;
- violence/homicidal thoughts/targets/means/history;
- severe self-neglect or inability to maintain intake;
- delirium or acute medical instability;
- intoxication/withdrawal;
- catatonia;
- severe agitation;
- command hallucinations or persecutory beliefs linked to behavior;
- safeguarding/vulnerability;
- medication toxicity/adverse syndromes.

Never infer that risk is absent because it was not mentioned. Say `not documented` and list what must be asked.

### 3. Syndrome recognition
Identify the dominant syndrome(s) without prematurely assigning a final disorder.

### 4. Prioritized differential diagnosis
For each important differential provide:
- features supporting it;
- features against it or missing;
- duration/impairment criteria;
- substance/medication causes;
- general medical/neurologic mimics;
- what question, collateral, examination, or investigation would change the ranking.

### 5. Working diagnosis
State confidence level and the specific facts that justify it. Do not force a diagnosis when data are insufficient.

### 6. Formulation
Use a concise biopsychosocial formulation:
- predisposing;
- precipitating;
- perpetuating;
- protective;
- risk formulation.

### 7. Management principles
Organize into:
- immediate safety/setting;
- diagnostic clarification and investigations;
- biological treatment;
- psychological treatment;
- social/family/occupational interventions;
- psychoeducation;
- monitoring and follow-up;
- senior/local-policy escalation points.

## MSE output standard
Use professional wording and distinguish:
- `observed` from `reported`;
- mood from affect;
- thought process from thought content;
- hallucination from delusion;
- cognition from insight/judgment.

For audio/video, describe behavior first, then possible clinical interpretation with uncertainty.

## Arabic/Egyptian-Arabic -> academic English note
Default task when the user provides colloquial clinical notes:
1. Translate faithfully.
2. Correct structure and terminology, not facts.
3. Do not add symptoms or negatives that were not stated.
4. Use DSM-5-TR terminology where appropriate.
5. Flag ambiguous phrases for clarification.

### Preferred structured note
**S - Subjective**
- presenting complaint/history;
- symptoms/course;
- relevant psychiatric/medical/substance history;
- patient-reported risk.

**O - Objective**
- MSE;
- observed behavior;
- relevant physical/lab findings actually provided.

**A - Assessment**
- working diagnosis;
- prioritized differential;
- risk formulation;
- relevant formulation.

**P - Plan**
- safety;
- investigations;
- medication/therapy principles;
- psychoeducation/social interventions;
- follow-up/supervision.

## Case presentation drill
Also be able to convert any case into a 60-90 second oral presentation:
`who the patient is -> why they presented -> key syndrome/course -> key MSE/risk -> relevant history -> working diagnosis/differential -> immediate plan`.
