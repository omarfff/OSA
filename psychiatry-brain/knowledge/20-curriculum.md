# 20 - Core Psychiatry Curriculum

## Phase A - Foundations that must become automatic
1. Psychiatric interview: rapport, confidentiality, presenting complaint, HPI, past psychiatric history, medication/medical/substance history, family/developmental/personal/occupational/relationship/forensic history, premorbid personality, function, collateral.
2. Mental Status Examination: appearance, behavior, psychomotor activity, speech, mood, affect, thought form/process, thought content, perception, cognition, insight, judgment.
3. Risk assessment: suicide/self-harm, violence, vulnerability, neglect, safeguarding, substance-related risk, access to means, protective factors, dynamic vs static risk, immediate safety plan.
4. Capacity/consent basics and documentation.
5. Biopsychosocial formulation and concise case presentation.
6. Organic/medical mimics and red flags before labeling symptoms psychiatric.

## Phase B - High-yield diagnostic blocks
- Depressive disorders.
- Bipolar and related disorders.
- Schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorders.
- Anxiety disorders.
- OCD and related disorders.
- Trauma- and stressor-related disorders.
- Personality disorders.
- Substance-related and addictive disorders.
- Delirium, major neurocognitive disorders, and differential of cognitive change.
- Somatic symptom and related disorders.
- Feeding/eating disorders.
- Sleep-wake disorders.
- Sexual dysfunctions/paraphilic disorders: exam-level terminology and safe clinical approach.
- Neurodevelopmental essentials: ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, tic disorders.
- Child/adolescent basics relevant to general psychiatry.
- Geriatric psychiatry essentials.
- Consultation-liaison psychiatry and medically ill patients.

## Phase C - Psychopharmacology
For every drug/class learn:
- mechanism only to the depth needed for clinical reasoning;
- indications and first-line role;
- common and dangerous adverse effects;
- baseline and follow-up monitoring;
- major interactions;
- pregnancy/lactation and age-specific cautions when relevant;
- discontinuation/withdrawal issues;
- toxicity syndromes and emergencies.

Core groups:
- SSRIs/SNRIs and other antidepressants.
- First- and second-generation antipsychotics.
- Lithium, valproate, carbamazepine, lamotrigine and other mood-stabilizing strategies.
- Benzodiazepines and non-benzodiazepine anxiolytic/sleep agents.
- ADHD medications.
- Substance-use pharmacotherapies.
- Dementia-related agents where relevant.

Must know emergencies/adverse syndromes:
- serotonin syndrome;
- neuroleptic malignant syndrome;
- lithium toxicity;
- severe withdrawal states;
- acute dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia;
- metabolic adverse effects;
- QTc and other high-risk monitoring concepts.

## Phase D - Emergency psychiatry
- Suicidal crisis/self-harm.
- Acute agitation and aggression.
- Acute psychosis.
- Mania with severe behavioral disturbance.
- Catatonia.
- Delirium.
- Alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal.
- Intoxication and polysubstance presentations.
- Severe depression with psychosis/catatonia or inability to maintain intake.
- ECT indications, principles, adverse effects, and peri-procedure knowledge.

## Phase E - Clinical pattern library
Maintain at least 50 core clinical patterns and expand continuously. Examples:
- auditory hallucinations + delusions + chronic functional decline -> schizophrenia-spectrum differential;
- reduced need for sleep + grandiosity + increased goal-directed activity -> mania;
- intrusive ego-dystonic thoughts + rituals -> OCD;
- acute fluctuating attention in medically unwell patient -> delirium;
- mutism + posturing + negativism/refusal -> catatonia;
- tremor + autonomic overactivity + hallucinations after alcohol cessation -> severe alcohol withdrawal;
- depressive episode + past hypomanic periods -> consider bipolar II before unipolar treatment.

## Phase F - Formal knowledge sources
Use current editions/current guidance where available, anchored to:
- DSM-5-TR.
- ICD-11 where useful.
- Maudsley Prescribing Guidelines in Psychiatry.
- Stahl's Essential Psychopharmacology and case-based companions.
- Oxford Handbook of Psychiatry.
- Kaplan & Sadock Synopsis of Psychiatry.
- 100 Cases in Psychiatry.
- Case Files Psychiatry.
- DSM-5/DSM-5-TR clinical cases/self-exam resources.
- NICE/local guidelines for depression, psychosis/schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, self-harm, and other relevant conditions.
