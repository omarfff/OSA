## Freshness / clinical-use rule

The book itself states that its recommendations reflect information available to the authors in March 2021 and that some advice will become outdated. Therefore:

1. Use Maudsley 14th for psychopharmacology reasoning, comparative prescribing, adverse-effect management, switching, monitoring principles, special populations, pharmacokinetics and exam reasoning.
2. For current treatment recommendations, safety warnings, licensed indications/doses, pregnancy restrictions, monitoring requirements, and regulatory changes, cross-check against current NICE guidance, current SmPC/regulatory sources, and local policy before presenting a point as current clinical guidance.
3. If Maudsley 14th conflicts with a newer authoritative guideline, explicitly label the difference and prefer the newer source for current practice while preserving Maudsley for historical/exam context.
4. Never imply this 2021 edition is the latest Maudsley edition.

## Chapter-to-domain map

### Chapter 1 — Schizophrenia and related psychoses
Primary domains: psychosis, psychopharmacology, emergency, risk.
High-yield areas: antipsychotic selection and dosing principles; high-dose and combined antipsychotics; monitoring and adverse-effect profiles; acute disturbance/rapid tranquillisation; LAIs; switching/stopping; EPS, akathisia, tardive dyskinesia, metabolic effects, QT prolongation, hyperprolactinaemia, sexual dysfunction, VTE; treatment-resistant schizophrenia; clozapine initiation, optimisation, interruption/restart, plasma levels and major adverse effects.
Pair with current NICE psychosis/schizophrenia guidance (CG178) for up-to-date practice.

### Chapter 2 — Bipolar disorder
Primary domains: mood, psychopharmacology, risk.
High-yield areas: lithium, valproate, carbamazepine, antipsychotics in bipolar disorder, monitoring, acute mania/hypomania, rapid cycling, bipolar depression, relapse prevention and stopping mood stabilisers.
Pair with current NICE bipolar guidance (CG185).

### Chapter 3 — Depression and anxiety disorders
Primary domains: mood, anxiety_ocd_trauma, psychopharmacology.
High-yield areas: antidepressant choice and minimum effective doses; treatment-resistant depression; augmentation; ketamine; psychotic depression; switching/stopping and withdrawal; ECT interactions; antidepressant adverse effects and drug interactions; anxiety-spectrum pharmacotherapy; benzodiazepine/z-drug/gabapentinoid dependence and discontinuation.
Pair with current NICE depression (NG222), GAD/panic (CG113), OCD/BDD (CG31), and other current disorder-specific guidance as relevant.

### Chapter 4 — Addictions and substance misuse
Primary domains: addiction, emergency, psychopharmacology, risk.
High-yield areas: alcohol dependence and withdrawal/delirium tremens; opioid dependence and substitution treatment; nicotine/smoking cessation; stimulant dependence; GHB/GBL; benzodiazepine misuse; synthetic cannabinoids; acute behavioural disturbance; interactions between drugs of misuse and prescribed psychotropics; substance misuse in pregnancy.
Cross-check against current national/local substance-use protocols before clinical use.

### Chapter 5 — Children and adolescents
Primary domains: child_adolescent, psychopharmacology, risk.
High-yield areas: developmental prescribing principles; depression, bipolar disorder, psychosis, anxiety, OCD/BDD, PTSD, ADHD, autism, tic/Tourette disorders, melatonin, rapid tranquillisation and paediatric psychotropic dosing.
Pair with current NICE ADHD (NG87) and current child/adolescent disorder-specific guidance.

### Chapter 6 — Prescribing in older people
Primary domains: geriatric, psychopharmacology, law_ethics.
High-yield areas: geriatric prescribing principles; dementia drugs; prescribing for physical conditions in dementia; behavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia; psychotropic dose ranges in older adults; covert administration; depression in older people.
Pair with current NICE dementia guidance (NG97).

### Chapter 7 — Pregnancy and breastfeeding
Primary domains: psychopharmacology, mood, law_ethics, risk.
High-yield areas: medication choice and risk discussion in pregnancy; antipsychotics, antidepressants and mood stabilisers; perinatal relapse considerations; breastfeeding and infant exposure.
Pair with current NICE antenatal/postnatal mental health guidance (CG192) and current regulatory pregnancy warnings.

### Chapter 8 — Hepatic and renal impairment
Primary domains: psychopharmacology, emergency.
High-yield areas: dose/agent selection in hepatic impairment and renal impairment; altered clearance; psychotropics requiring extra caution or avoidance.
Always verify current SmPC/renal-hepatic dosing before clinical use.

### Chapter 9 — Other psychiatric conditions
Primary domains: psychotherapy, emergency, geriatric, child_adolescent, mood.
High-yield areas: borderline personality disorder, eating disorders, delirium.
Use as prescribing context rather than a replacement for disorder-specific full management guidance.

### Chapter 10 — Psychiatric symptoms in other medical disorders
Primary domains: psychopharmacology, emergency, geriatric.
High-yield areas: HIV, epilepsy, 22q11.2 deletion syndrome, learning disability, Huntington disease, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson disease, atrial fibrillation, bariatric surgery and end-of-life psychiatry.
Prioritise drug-disease and drug-drug interaction reasoning.

### Chapter 11 — Pharmacokinetics
Primary domains: psychopharmacology.
High-yield areas: plasma-level monitoring; interpreting concentrations; clozapine plasma levels; CYP interactions; smoking and psychotropics; alcohol interactions.
This chapter is a core source for pharmacokinetic viva/MCQ generation.

### Chapter 12 — Other substances
Primary domains: addiction, psychopharmacology.
High-yield areas: caffeine and nicotine, including interaction effects relevant to psychotropics.

### Chapter 13 — Psychotropic drugs in special conditions
Primary domains: emergency, psychopharmacology, risk.
High-yield areas: overdose, driving, perioperative psychotropics and surgery.
Cross-check current toxicology/perioperative/local emergency policy.

### Chapter 14 — Miscellany
Primary domains: psychopharmacology, law_ethics, formulation.
High-yield areas: adherence; restarting medication after non-adherence; biochemical/haematological effects; psychiatric effects of non-psychotropics; off-label prescribing; Mental Health Act context; IM injection sites; IV psychotropics.
Legal content is UK-specific and must not be substituted for Egyptian or Saudi law.

- Do not invent a dose, monitoring interval, contraindication, or regulatory warning that is not grounded in the provided chapter extract or an explicitly retrieved current source.
