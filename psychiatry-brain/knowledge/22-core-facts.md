# 22 - High-Confidence Core Psychiatry Anchors

These are compact factual anchors for the local study model. They are not a substitute for current full DSM-5-TR criteria, prescribing references, local policy, or senior clinical supervision.

## Mental Status Examination
- **Mood** is the patient's sustained, predominant internal emotional state and is primarily **subjectively reported** by the patient (for example: depressed, anxious, irritable, euthymic).
- **Affect** is the **observed external expression** of emotion during the interview. Describe range, intensity, reactivity, stability, and congruence with stated mood/thought content when relevant.
- Mood and affect are related but not interchangeable. A patient can report a depressed mood while showing a restricted, blunted, labile, or mood-congruent affect.
- **Thought process/form** describes how ideas are connected and organized (for example: linear, circumstantial, tangential, flight of ideas, loosening of associations).
- **Thought content** describes what the person is thinking about (for example: delusions, obsessions, suicidal ideas, preoccupations).
- **Perception** includes hallucinations and other perceptual disturbances; hallucinations are perceptions occurring without an external stimulus.
- **Insight** concerns awareness and understanding of illness/symptoms and need for care; **judgment** concerns decision-making and ability to act appropriately in context.

## Depressive disorders
- A DSM-5-TR major depressive episode requires **five or more symptoms during the same 2-week period**, representing a change from previous functioning, and at least one symptom must be depressed mood or loss of interest/pleasure (with developmental wording differences for children/adolescents).
- Diagnosis also requires clinically significant distress or impairment and appropriate exclusion of substance/medication effects, medical causes, and manic/hypomanic history as applicable.
- Never infer suicide risk level from the diagnosis alone. Suicide risk assessment requires direct assessment of ideation, intent, plan, means/access, past behavior, dynamic/static risk factors, protective factors, and current context.

## Bipolar spectrum timing anchors
- A manic episode has a distinct period of abnormally and persistently elevated, expansive, or irritable mood **and increased activity/energy lasting at least 1 week** (or any duration if hospitalization is necessary), with required associated symptoms and marked impairment/psychosis/hospitalization criteria as applicable.
- A hypomanic episode lasts **at least 4 consecutive days**, is observable as an unequivocal change from usual functioning, but is not severe enough by itself to cause marked impairment or require hospitalization and has no psychotic features.

## Psychosis and mood disorders
- Do not diagnose schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, or a mood disorder with psychotic features from the presence of psychosis alone. Longitudinal timing of mood episodes relative to psychosis is essential.
- A history of mania or hypomania must be actively considered before assuming recurrent depression is unipolar.

## Delirium and cognitive change
- Delirium is characterized by an **acute disturbance in attention and awareness**, developing over a short period and tending to fluctuate, with additional cognitive disturbance and evidence of a physiological/substance/medical cause.
- Acute fluctuating inattention in a medically unwell patient is a medical red flag; do not label it as a primary psychiatric disorder without evaluating delirium and medical causes.

## OCD terminology
- **Obsessions** are recurrent intrusive thoughts, urges, or images experienced as unwanted and usually causing anxiety/distress.
- **Compulsions** are repetitive behaviors or mental acts performed in response to an obsession or rigid rules, aimed at reducing distress or preventing a feared event, even when not realistically connected or clearly excessive.
- OCD and obsessive-compulsive personality disorder are distinct conditions; OCPD concerns a pervasive personality pattern of orderliness, perfectionism, and control rather than true obsessions/compulsions being required.

## Medication/emergency anchors
- Akathisia is a subjective sense of inner restlessness with observable motor restlessness and can be mistaken for anxiety or agitation.
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome and serotonin syndrome are medical emergencies with overlapping autonomic/neuromuscular features but different typical medication exposures and examination patterns; do not diagnose either from one symptom alone.
- Lithium toxicity must be considered when compatible neurologic/GI symptoms occur in a patient taking lithium, especially with dehydration, renal impairment, interacting medication, or dose changes.

## Evidence discipline
- If the requested fact is not represented in the local knowledge and the model is uncertain, say so rather than inventing a criterion, duration, dose, test result, or treatment rule.
- A board-style mnemonic is not a diagnostic criterion unless explicitly identified as such.
