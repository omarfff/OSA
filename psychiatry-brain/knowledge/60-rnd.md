# 60 - Psychiatry AI, R&D, Invention, and Patent Track

## Mission
Use the fellowship years not only to pass exams but to identify clinically important psychiatry problems that could become AI systems, devices, workflows, research projects, patents, or investable products.

## R&D domains
- Psychiatric clinical documentation and Arabic/Egyptian-Arabic -> structured English notes.
- Multimodal MSE assistance using text, speech/prosody, and behavior/movement observations.
- Suicide/self-harm safety workflows and clinician decision support.
- Medication adverse-effect/monitoring support.
- Longitudinal symptom and treatment-response tracking.
- Differential-diagnosis training systems.
- Simulation systems for psychiatric interviewing and viva/oral exams.
- Objective structured educational benchmarks for psychiatry LLMs.
- Workflow tools for psychiatric residents, clinics, wards, and emergency departments.
- Measurement devices/sensors only where a clinically meaningful signal and validation pathway exist.

## Idea evaluation rubric
For every proposed idea score:
1. clinical pain/problem severity;
2. frequency of the problem;
3. current workaround and its weakness;
4. measurable input signal/data availability;
5. technical feasibility;
6. safety risk if wrong;
7. need for clinician-in-the-loop;
8. regulatory/ethics burden;
9. novelty/prior-art risk;
10. validation study design;
11. buyer/user and implementation workflow;
12. defensibility: proprietary data, workflow integration, model/evaluation method, device/IP, or evidence.

## Patent discipline
Do not call something patentable merely because it is interesting. Before using the word `patentable`, distinguish:
- idea;
- potentially novel implementation;
- prior-art search needed;
- provisional-patent candidate;
- validated invention.

Document problem, novelty claim, system architecture, inputs/outputs, decision logic, safety controls, alternatives, and experimental evidence.

## PsychiatryBench inspiration
Use multidimensional evaluation rather than a single accuracy score. Relevant model competencies include:
- diagnostic reasoning;
- treatment decision-making;
- treatment follow-up;
- classification;
- management planning;
- clinical approach;
- foundational knowledge;
- sequential reasoning;
- MCQ/EMI performance;
- safety and uncertainty handling.

## Research notebook rule
Every clinical frustration noticed during training can be captured as:
`problem -> current workflow -> failure mode -> proposed intervention -> measurable outcome -> data needed -> validation design -> safety risks -> IP/business potential`.

The study brain should continuously connect clinical learning to research questions while keeping real-patient care under supervision and separating educational hypotheses from validated clinical tools.
