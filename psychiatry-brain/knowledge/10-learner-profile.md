## Preferred learning mode
- Video-first whenever an appropriate learning resource exists.
- Minimal required reading; convert long chapters into high-yield structured notes.
- Practical, low-pressure learning rather than dense memorization.
- Arabic explanation first when needed, with the exact English psychiatric terms alongside it.
- Medical English should be strengthened progressively without making heavy English reading the bottleneck.
- Daily, concrete tasks rather than vague study plans.
- Heavy repetition through MCQs, cases, oral questioning, rapid recall, and spaced review.
- Learn from clinical patterns, not isolated definitions.
- Use visual tables, differential-diagnosis grids, medication comparisons, and exam traps.
- Prioritize understanding of why an answer is correct and why close alternatives are wrong.
- Prefer MCQs, clinical vignettes, videos, and supervised real clinical exposure over passive textbook reading.
- Avoid designing the whole pathway around unnecessarily heavy OSCE/CASC/PACES-style burdens when the target credential does not require them; still train the real clinical skills those formats test.

## Foundation milestones
A useful early-residency bootstrapping target from the existing study system is:
- roughly 70-100 foundational psychiatry concepts made automatic;
- roughly 300 carefully explained core MCQs before expanding the question bank;
- 40-50 high-yield clinical patterns;
- automatic competence in interview, MSE, risk, formulation, differential diagnosis, medication safety, and psychiatric emergencies.

These are milestones, not ceilings. The long-term system should expand well beyond them.

## Teaching style
For each topic, teach in this order when practical:
1. One-sentence clinical picture.
2. Core DSM-5-TR criteria/structure.
3. What must never be missed.
4. Common differentials and how to distinguish them.
5. MSE/risk implications.
6. First-line management principles.
7. Medication adverse effects/monitoring if relevant.
8. Exam traps.
9. One mini-case.
10. 5-10 retrieval questions.

## Progress philosophy
The goal is not passive reading. The learner should become able to:
- recognize a syndrome quickly;
- conduct and document a psychiatric interview;
- perform a structured MSE;
- assess suicide/violence/medical risk;
- generate a prioritized differential;
- formulate biopsychosocially;
- propose a safe management plan under supervision;
- present the case in concise academic English;
- answer board-style questions under time pressure;
- identify clinically meaningful research/invention problems during training and convert them into testable projects.
