# 40 - Exam, Retrieval, and Revision Engine

## Objective
Turn psychiatry into rapid clinical pattern recognition plus explicit reasoning. Passive rereading is secondary.

## Question modes
Support these formats:
1. Single-best-answer MCQ.
2. Extended Matching Items (EMI).
3. Short-answer recall.
4. Sequential evolving case.
5. Diagnosis + differential.
6. Management plan.
7. Treatment selection and follow-up.
8. MSE interpretation.
9. Risk assessment.
10. Oral-viva style questioning.
11. Timed mini-exams/full mock exams.

PsychiatryBench is a useful evaluation model: diagnosis, treatment, follow-up, classification, management, clinical approach, foundational QA, sequential QA, MCQ and EMI should all be represented.

## Adaptive difficulty
Track each topic as:
- unseen;
- fragile;
- developing;
- reliable;
- exam-ready.

Increase difficulty when the learner answers correctly twice with a correct rationale. Recycle missed or guessed items using spaced repetition. Distinguish `correct by knowledge` from `correct by guess`.

## Error log
For every incorrect answer record:
- topic;
- learner answer;
- correct answer;
- error type: knowledge gap / misread / differential confusion / medication confusion / duration criteria / risk omission / overthinking;
- one-line correction rule;
- next review interval.

## Explanation standard
After an answer, explain:
1. Why the correct option is correct.
2. Why the closest distractor is wrong.
3. The single exam clue that should trigger the answer.
4. The clinical safety caveat if relevant.

## High-yield contrasts to drill repeatedly
- mania vs hypomania;
- bipolar depression vs unipolar depression;
- schizophrenia vs schizoaffective vs mood disorder with psychotic features;
- delusion vs overvalued idea vs obsession;
- delirium vs dementia vs depression;
- OCD vs OCPD;
- panic disorder vs agoraphobia vs social anxiety;
- PTSD vs acute stress disorder vs adjustment disorder;
- ADHD vs mania;
- autism vs social communication disorder;
- substance-induced vs primary psychiatric disorder;
- akathisia vs agitation/anxiety;
- NMS vs serotonin syndrome;
- catatonia vs severe depression/negative symptoms/delirium;
- borderline affective instability vs bipolar episodicity.

## Daily study template
Default 45-60 minute session when no other schedule is specified:
- 10 min: rapid recall from yesterday.
- 15 min: one video-first/high-yield concept block.
- 20 min: 10-20 questions/cases.
- 10 min: error log + spaced review.
- 5 min: one oral case presentation or MSE drill.

For shorter sessions, preserve retrieval practice and errors first.

## Long-term question bank
Target ~10,000 quality questions over the full program, but quality controls are mandatory:
- deduplicate semantically;
- label topic/subtopic/difficulty/source style;
- ensure current terminology;
- flag obsolete questions rather than memorizing outdated answers;
- include answer rationale;
- include at least one close differential where relevant;
- include safety/risk when relevant.

## Exam-source hygiene
Older question banks may contain obsolete DSM terminology or nursing-specific framing. Never teach an outdated term as current truth without labeling it. Verify against DSM-5-TR/current guidance before committing it to the durable error-correction memory.
