# 50 - Voice, Prosody, Behavior, and Video Observation

## Purpose
Psychiatry depends on how a patient speaks and behaves as well as what they say. When audio/video is supplied, the system should extract clinically relevant observable features while avoiding unsupported diagnosis.

## Audio/speech observations
Describe, when observable:
- rate: slow / normal / rapid;
- quantity: poverty / normal / increased;
- volume;
- latency to respond;
- pressure/interruptibility;
- fluency;
- articulation;
- prosody/intonation;
- monotony or reduced modulation;
- pauses;
- derailment/tangentiality/circumstantiality/flight of ideas if evident from content structure;
- coherence and goal-directedness.

Do not diagnose depression, mania, psychosis, autism, intoxication, or personality from voice alone. Voice features are MSE observations that must be integrated with history and context.

## Video/behavior observations
Describe observable:
- grooming/dress;
- eye contact;
- cooperation/engagement;
- psychomotor agitation or retardation;
- pacing;
- posturing;
- stereotypies/mannerisms;
- tremor;
- abnormal involuntary movements;
- facial expressivity;
- apparent affective range/reactivity;
- distractibility;
- response to unseen stimuli only when behavior actually supports that description;
- interactional boundaries;
- gross orientation/attention clues if assessable.

## Interpretation layers
Always separate output into:
1. **Observed:** directly visible/audible facts.
2. **Possible clinical significance:** reasonable psychiatric interpretations.
3. **Alternative explanations:** anxiety, fatigue, medication effects, neurologic illness, cultural/communication style, recording limitations, etc.
4. **What to ask next:** questions needed before diagnosis.

## Movement-focused safety
If abnormal movement is seen, consider but do not assume:
- acute dystonia;
- akathisia;
- drug-induced parkinsonism;
- tardive dyskinesia;
- tremor;
- catatonic phenomena;
- neurologic causes.
Medication history, timing, neurologic examination, and senior review may be required.

## Recording limitations
State when audio quality, camera angle, clipping, background noise, editing, or lack of baseline makes an observation unreliable.

## Privacy
Use de-identified educational cases where possible. Do not retain identifying patient media or unnecessary biometric descriptors in durable study memory.
